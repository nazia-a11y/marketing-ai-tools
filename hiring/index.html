        How this works

        Fixed formulas, published sources, and an honest note on what a planning tool cannot tell you.

        1. Your hiring plan becomes a headcount for each of the next 24 months. Nobody starts before the month you chose, then numbers grow in a straight line to 12 months and 24 months.
        2. Each person costs their salary plus the employer contributions set by law in that country for 2026, such as social security and required pensions. This is the same on either route, because an employer of record passes these costs on.
        3. On top of pay, employer of record costs a monthly fee for each person. Your own company costs a one-off setup, a monthly running cost and payroll for each person.
        4. The tool finds when your own company becomes cheaper to run and whether it pays back its setup cost within 24 months. If your first hire is far enough away, it checks whether you can skip employer of record and set up first.
        5. Three checks decide the route: cost, risk and strategy. Risks such as local sales staff, licences or country rules that limit employer of record can make your own company necessary sooner.
        6. All numbers are calculated in your browser with fixed formulas, never by AI. Claude only reviews the finished figures, and reviews are kept for 24 hours.

        Where the figures come from. Employer contributions: 2026 rates and ceilings from government sources (HMRC, gov.ie, Deutsche Rentenversicherung and Techniker Krankenkasse, URSSAF rates published by CCI Paris, the Dutch government gazette, Seguridad Social, the official ZUS ceiling notice, the CPF Board, the ATO and Revenue NSW, the Government of Canada and the Government of Ontario), checked against PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries. They cover statutory costs only. Employer of record fees: about $599 per person a month, the common published starting fee. Setup and running costs: typical figures from local advisers and formation firms. Country rules: legal briefings from Taylor Wessing, Addleshaw Goddard, Le Global, CMS, Lawants, Dudkowiak, Jackson Lewis and the Government of Ontario.

        This is general guidance for planning, not legal or tax advice. Confirm the route with an adviser in the country before you act.
