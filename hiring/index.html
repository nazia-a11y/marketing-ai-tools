      How this works, and what it cannot tell you
      1. Your hiring plan becomes a headcount for each of the next 24 months. Nobody starts before the month you chose, then numbers grow in a straight line to 12 months and 24 months.
      2. Each person costs their salary plus the employer contributions set by law in that country for 2026, such as social security and required pensions. This is the same on either route, because an employer of record passes these costs on.
      3. On top of pay, employer of record costs a monthly fee for each person. Your own company costs a one-off setup, a monthly running cost and payroll for each person. Typical figures are filled in for each country, and you can replace them with real quotes.
      4. The tool finds when your own company becomes cheaper to run and whether it pays back its setup cost within 24 months. If your first hire is far enough away, it checks whether you can skip employer of record and set up first.
      5. Three checks decide the route: cost, risk and strategy. Risks such as local sales staff, licences or country rules that limit employer of record can make your own company necessary sooner than costs alone suggest.
      6. All numbers are calculated in your browser with fixed formulas. They are never produced by AI. Claude is only asked to review the finished figures, and reviews are kept for 24 hours, so asking again about the same figures is free.

      Where the figures come from. Employer contributions: 2026 rates and ceilings from government sources (HMRC, gov.ie, Deutsche Rentenversicherung and Techniker Krankenkasse, URSSAF rates published by CCI Paris, the Dutch government gazette, Seguridad Social, the official ZUS ceiling notice, the CPF Board, the ATO and Revenue NSW, the Government of Canada and the Government of Ontario), checked against PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries. They cover statutory costs only, not optional benefits, sector agreements or workplace accident insurance where the rate depends on the sector. Employer of record fees: about $599 per person a month, the common published starting fee, converted at rough exchange rates. Setup times and costs, and running costs: typical figures from local advisers and formation firms, so treat them as estimates. Country rules on employer of record: legal briefings from Taylor Wessing (Germany), Addleshaw Goddard (France), Le Global (Ireland), CMS (Netherlands), Lawants (Spain), Dudkowiak (Poland), Jackson Lewis (Singapore) and the Government of Ontario.

      This is general guidance for planning, not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by country and change over time, so confirm the route with an adviser in the country before you act.
