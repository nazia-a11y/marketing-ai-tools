Hiring in a new country? You can employ people through an employer of record, which employs them for you, or set up your own local company. This tool shows which route fits your plans, when to switch, and what each costs over 24 months, then asks Claude for a plain-English review.

    How this works, and what it cannot tell you
    1. Your hiring plan is turned into a headcount for each of the next 24 months, in a straight line from now to 12 months to 24 months.
    2. Employer of record costs the monthly fee for each person. Your own company costs a one-off setup, a fixed monthly running cost and payroll for each person. Salaries and employer taxes are the same either way, so they are left out.
    3. The tool finds the month when your own company becomes cheaper to run, and checks whether switching then would save money over 24 months once setup is paid for. Setting up takes about 2.5 to 5 months depending on the type of country, so employer of record covers the gap.
    4. It then applies three checks: cost, risk and strategy. Risks such as local sales staff or licences can make your own company necessary earlier than the costs alone suggest.
    5. All numbers are calculated in your browser with fixed formulas. They are never produced by AI. Claude is only asked to review the finished figures, and reviews are kept for 24 hours, so asking again about the same figures is free.

    Where the figures come from. Employer of record fees: $499 to $699 per person a month from the main providers (Teamed, from provider pricing pages, September 2026). Setting up a company: about €5,000 to €20,000 in Western Europe and $15,000 to $20,000 in most countries, taking 2 to 6 months (Teamed, Lano, WorkMotion). Running a company: about €1,000 to €4,000 a month before salaries (Teamed). Costs often cross over at around 5 to 10 people in simpler countries, higher elsewhere, and about 30% to 50% higher where you do not speak the local language (GoGlobal, WorkMotion, Teamed). The three checks follow published guidance on moving from employer of record to your own company.

    This is general guidance for planning, not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by country and change over time, so confirm the route with an adviser in the country before you act.

    Sources: GoGlobal on moving from employer of record to your own company, Teamed cost comparison, WorkMotion decision framework, Lano on setup cost and time, The HR Director on tax presence risk.
